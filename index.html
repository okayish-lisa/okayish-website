Why It Works

There's actual science here. We'll keep it brief.

Three things happen when you use Okay*ish. None of them require you to believe in anything.

1

Writing it down changes it.

When you put something into words, it shifts from something happening to you to something you're describing. Psychologists call this affect labeling. You probably call it getting it out of your head. Both are right. Either way, something loosens.

2

A different angle is not the same as advice.

The situation doesn't change. But the way you're holding it can. Okay*ish offers a fresh way to look at what happened. Not to convince you of anything, just to give you somewhere else to stand for a second. That's usually enough.

3

The peak of a bad feeling lasts about 90 seconds.

Here's something most people don't know: the physiological peak of an emotional response lasts roughly 60 to 90 seconds. After that, you're mostly just re-triggering it. By the time you've read your kit, that window has usually passed. You didn't suppress anything. The moment just ran its course.

The Science

Grounded in cognitive reappraisal research.

Okay*ish is built on a well-established body of research into how changing the way we interpret a situation changes how we feel about it.

Affect Labeling

Putting feelings into words reduces emotional intensity

Writing down what happened isn't just venting. Research found that labeling emotions with words measurably reduces activity in the amygdala, creating an immediate calming effect on emotional intensity, even without any deliberate effort to regulate.

Read the study →

Cognitive Reappraisal

Reappraisal is more effective for regulating emotions than moods

This PMC study found that cognitive reappraisal specifically targets the appraisal stage, making it highly effective for in-the-moment emotional regulation, with longer-lasting effects than attention-based strategies.

Read the study →

Humor and Coping

Humor functions as a cognitive reappraisal strategy

Research on humor as a coping mechanism shows it operates through the same cognitive pathway as reappraisal, creating psychological distance from a stressor without requiring deliberate effort. Humor isn't avoidance; it's a well-studied route to a lighter frame.

Read the study →

Emotional Timing

Brief engagement at the moment of distress prevents escalation

Affect labeling works as implicit emotion regulation, meaning the brief act of putting a difficult moment into words can interrupt the escalation cycle before it compounds. The window for intervention is short, and early engagement makes a measurable difference.

Read the study →

Neuroimaging

Cognitive reappraisal and emotion: a meta-analysis of neuroimaging studies

Reappraisal recruits frontal and parietal control regions to modulate emotional responding in the amygdala, a well-replicated mechanism across 48 neuroimaging studies reviewed in this meta-analysis.

Read the study →

AI-Assisted Reappraisal

Large language models are capable of offering cognitive reappraisal, if guided

Research from arXiv demonstrates that LLMs can deliver clinically grounded cognitive reappraisals when structured appropriately. This is the mechanism Okay*ish is built on, with the addition of personalized humor calibration, structured session design, and privacy-first architecture that general AI tools don't offer.

4. How Your Information Is Processed

Legal version

Processing Architecture

Generating Your Kit: When you submit Your Words, the App sends them through our server to our AI service provider, OpenAI, together with your style preferences expressed in plain words for that single request. No account identifier, email address, name, or device identifier is included in the request.

Classification and Safety: As part of the same request, the AI provider assigns your situation a weight (everyday, heavy, or crisis) and a broad situation type, and generates your response. A second automated check reviews the response before it is shown to you. Where a situation is classified as a possible crisis, the App displays crisis resources instead of a comedic response.

Learning Signals: Only deliberate actions you take on a kit (such as keeping or passing on a piece, or copying a one-liner) update your Preference Totals. Inaction, closing the App, or starting a new moment is never recorded as a signal. Heavy and crisis moments do not generate signals.

AI Provider Retention: OpenAI processes Your Words to generate your response under its API data terms. Under those terms, OpenAI does not use API content to train its models by default, and may retain API requests for up to thirty (30) days for abuse and misuse monitoring before deletion, unless longer retention is required by law. This retention is outside our control. Requests are not linked to your identity by us. We will update this policy if a zero-retention arrangement is put in place.

Service Providers: We use the following categories of service providers, each bound by contractual confidentiality and data protection obligations: OpenAI (AI generation, United States); Supabase (account, preference backup, usage count, and feedback storage, hosted in Canada); RevenueCat (subscription status, United States); Apple and Google (app distribution, sign-in, and payment); and email and hosting providers needed to operate the App and support.

We may also disclose information where required by law, to protect the rights or safety of any person, or in connection with a merger, acquisition, or sale of assets, in which case this policy will continue to apply to the information transferred.

Plain language

What Happens When You Use the App

Here is the full life of what you type, step by step:

3. Description of Service

Legal version

Service Overview

The App uses artificial intelligence to generate comedic reframes of everyday situations you describe. For everyday moments, the App provides a "kit" consisting of a short reframe, a one-liner, and a song suggestion. For situations the App classifies as heavy, it provides a gentler response. For situations it classifies as a possible crisis, it provides crisis resources instead of a comedic response.

The App is intended for general wellbeing and lighthearted perspective only. It uses a third-party AI service provider to process your input and generate responses, and maintains preference totals to tailor its style to you over time, as described in our Privacy Policy.

Creating an account is optional. Some features, such as keeping your preferences when you change devices, may require an account.

We may add, change, or remove features at any time. We will provide reasonable notice of material changes to core functionality.

Plain language

What Okay*ish Does

You tell Okay*ish what happened, in your own words. It gives you back a kit: a funny new way to look at the moment, a one-liner you can text a friend, and a song that fits.

If what you share is heavy, like grief or a loss, you get something gentler instead of jokes. If it sounds like you might be in danger, you get crisis support info instead.

The app uses AI to write your kit, and it learns what makes you laugh over time. (Our Privacy Policy explains how, and what we do and don't keep.)
